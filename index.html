A Policy Benchmarking Demo

AI in Financial Policy

A general inspection of policy attention and narrative on artificial intelligence in international banking regulation and supervision — how attention has evolved, where risks are framed, and where standard-setters diverge from academic literature.

Scroll

Scope and data collection

387 documents were collected from five international bodies in financial regulation and supervision, covering 2018 to 2025. Each body's publications page served as the primary source: the BIS, for instance, maintains an "AI at BIS" stream, with equivalent AI-tagged collections drawn from FATF, IMF, OECD, and the World Bank. Inclusion required that a document mention "artificial intelligence" or "machine learning". Documents centred on topics outside the financial system, such as labour or health AI applications, were excluded.

The number of documents that mention artificial intelligence or machine learning grows over 2018 to 2025, with a sharp acceleration after ChatGPT's release. The three post-ChatGPT years account for roughly 70 % of the database, with 48, 86, and 112 documents in 2023, 2024, and 2025 respectively. Hovering on any bar reveals the per-body breakdown for that year.

Policy papers and academic papers each account for roughly a third of the database. Another 17 % consists of speeches and commentary, and the rest comes from stocktakes, formal standards, and country assessments. The supervisory work of these institutions and their internal research are receiving comparable attention to AI, though the content of that attention differ as shown next.

Policy attention to AI

Two complementary readings of the same documents — one counts words, the other reads meaning.

1. Traditional reading

Experts pick a list of keywords and count how often they appear. Across these documents, the keyword method tells a sharp story: negligible attention pre-2022, then a jump from 2023 onward.

The catch: this reading captures a vocabulary shift more than an attention shift. Tier 1 GenAI terms — "foundation model," "large language model," "GenAI" — simply did not exist as a regulator's vocabulary before 2022.

2. Semantic reading

Transformer embeddings score every paragraph by its semantic proximity to a curated set of AI "anchor" sentences. The story changes: AI-adjacent attention was already substantial before the GenAI wave, and rose steadily.

Robust across three sensitivity thresholds. The tightening of the confidence band after 2022 suggests attention has become more focused — earlier years were dispersed across many framings.
