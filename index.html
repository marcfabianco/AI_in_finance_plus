AI in Financial Policy

Section 01

The corpus

Which documents we read, why we chose them, and how the corpus is distributed over time and across types.

We include documents that international bodies themselves classify as AI-relevant. The BIS, for instance, curates an AI at BIS publications stream — we ingest all documents, speeches, and notes filed under that classification.

Equivalent classifications are used at the FSB, IMF, World Bank, FATF, and OECD. Insurers (IAIS) and securities (IOSCO) are out of scope; gambling-adjacent AI literature is also excluded.

Section 02

Policy attention to AI

Two complementary readings of the same corpus — one counts words, the other reads meaning.

1. Traditional reading

Experts pick a list of keywords and count how often they appear. On this corpus, the keyword method tells a sharp story: negligible attention pre-2022, then a jump from 2023 onward.

The catch: this reading captures a vocabulary shift more than an attention shift. Tier 1 GenAI terms — "foundation model," "large language model," "GenAI" — simply did not exist as a regulator's vocabulary before 2022.

2. Semantic reading

Transformer embeddings score every paragraph by its semantic proximity to a curated set of AI "anchor" sentences. The story changes: AI-adjacent attention was already substantial before the GenAI wave, and rose steadily.

Robust across three sensitivity thresholds. The tightening of the confidence band after 2022 suggests attention has become more focused — earlier years were dispersed across many framings.
